### 1.2 PIT discipline
All forward-return joins use `pandas.merge_asof` with `direction="backward"` for the current reference price and `direction="forward"` for the price at `t + horizon`, tolerance capped at `2x horizon` (see `scripts/lib.py::add_forward_mid`) -- no reference price is ever looked up beyond its actual arrival timestamp, and unmatched (missing/stale) forward prices are dropped (NaN) rather than filled. Book reconstruction (`scripts/book_reconstruct.py`) replays raw diff/snapshot events in arrival order; the BBO stream never touches the deep-book dict, so L1 and depth-5bps features are built from genuinely separate reconstructions (relevant to the B1 vs round-2-L1 comparison below). Execution-filter tests (`mp3_exec_filters.py`) use only **actual observed large-trade prints** as the entry events (top-1pct notional trades), not synthetic decisions from any live alpha -- the script's own header flags that `market_physics_v3`'s window (2026-08-15 onward) does not temporally overlap with the frozen Track-A alphas' historical decision logs, so this is a standalone feasibility test of the filter mechanism, not a literal replay of any Track-A alpha's entries.

### 1.3 Declustering
`N_raw` = every qualifying tick/trade/event (heavily autocorrelated/overlapping). `N_independent` = a greedy non-overlapping sampler (`scripts/lib.py::decluster_mask`) that keeps a point only once at least one full horizon has elapsed since the last kept point -- i.e. no two kept observations share a forward-return window. All `gross_bps` figures are computed on the independent (declustered) sample; `N_raw` is reported alongside only as a scale/liquidity indicator, never as the basis for a significance claim.

### 1.4 Effect definition and cost assumptions
`gross_bps` = top-minus-bottom decile (or tercile/binary, depending on candidate) spread of forward return conditional on the signal -- **a single-leg effect size, not a round-trip P&L number**, following the same convention W7 used in round 2. Cost assumption for this round (per brief): `taker_fee_bps = 5.0`/leg, `maker_fee_bps = 2.0`/leg (this project's `execution_simulator` defaults) -- so a full round-trip (open+close) costs **10bps taker / 4bps maker**, and `net_maker_bps = gross_bps - 4.0`, `net_taker_bps = gross_bps - 10.0` throughout. Note this taker/maker split is *stricter* than W7 round 2's ~1.5bps maker-leg assumption used for the "closest to cost" comparison quoted in the brief -- the two are not directly interchangeable; round-3 net numbers below are always computed on the 2/5bps-per-leg convention.

### 1.5 Execution-filter framing
Per this round's explicit priority, five candidates (`E1`-`E5`) reframe existing/round-2 book-state signals as a **go/no-go entry gate** on real large-trade prints: `execution_filter_delta_bps` = (mean post-entry aligned return under the filter) minus (unfiltered baseline), and `PF_baseline`/`PF_filtered` are the profit factors (`scripts/lib.py::profit_factor`) of the sign-aligned position with/without the gate. A positive, stable delta across both periods and both venues would be the actionable finding; see section 4.

## 4. Top findings (prose)

**Headline: zero PROMISING mechanisms this round.** Of 31 candidates, none clear the round's 4bps maker / 10bps taker round-trip cost bar with an adequate, stable sample. This is consistent with rounds 1 and 2's overall pattern (round 2's best standalone finding, depth-imbalance/microprice-offset/OFI at 0.7-1.63bps gross, was itself only "closest to cost," not over it).

### market_physics_v3 (real N, the only source that can support a status claim beyond DATA_LIMITED)

1. **The round-2 L1 edge does not survive moving one layer deeper into the book.** `B1_DEPTH5_IMBALANCE` replicates round-2's #1 standalone finding (resting-depth imbalance) but built on depth-within-5bps instead of L1-only, and the edge collapses roughly 10-40x, from round-2's 0.7-1.6bps gross down to a median 0.09bps here. Combined with `E5_DEPTH_IMBALANCE_L1_FILTER`'s period-inconsistent sign when the *actual* L1 version is reframed as an execution filter, the picture is that round-2's edge is a genuinely thin, L1-specific effect that does not generalize to nearby constructions -- not a robust regime feature.

2. **Execution-filter reframing (this round's stated priority) did not find a usable filter.** Of `E1`-`E5`, only `E2_IMBALANCE_ALIGNED_FILTER` (gate large-trade entries to when the book already agrees with the trade's direction) shows a consistently positive delta (6/8 cells, median +0.067bps) -- but the magnitude is too small to matter, and `E5` (the direct reframing of round-2's headline finding) flips sign between the A_early and B_late periods, which by itself disqualifies it as a stable filter. `E1` and `E4` are net DEAD (delta ~0 or negative in most cells), `E3` never had enough absorption events to test (max 12 independent observations per cell). Net: existing large-trade entries already carry decent post-entry drift on their own (PF_baseline 4.4-32.9, gross 0.34-0.71bps unfiltered) -- conditioning on any of these five signals does not reliably improve that baseline enough to change the conclusion.

3. **Two fully sign-consistent, wrong-direction findings worth flagging even though they're sub-cost.** `T2_STEALTH_ABSORPTION` (large trades absorbed without immediate impact) is followed by a small REVERSAL, not the continuation that round-2's absorption story would predict (15/15 valid cells agree, median -0.60bps). `T4_INTERTRADE_COOLDOWN` (seconds since the last big trade) is the single cleanest result in the whole sweep -- 18/18 cells agree in sign, more recent big-trade activity consistently precedes HIGHER forward volatility, not lower (median -0.45bps on a "time-since -> vol" mapping). Both are ~15-30x too small to trade standalone, but both are candidates for the execution-filter framing in a future round (e.g., avoid entries immediately after a large print, or treat post-absorption windows as mean-reversion rather than momentum triggers) -- neither was tested as a filter this round.

4. **`T3_TRADEFLOW_LEAD_QUOTE` is a diagnostic, not a signal, and needs a follow-up before it can be scored.** Across 36 cells testing both causal directions, the quote-leads-flow direction dominates in effect size over flow-leads-quote in nearly every venue/period/horizon -- i.e. the book moves first and trade prints follow, not the reverse. That is a real, repeated pattern, but the outcome variable (an imbalance-ratio delta or a signed-trade count) was never mapped to an actual forward bps return, so it cannot be marked WEAK/DEAD/PROMISING as-is; it's BLOCKED pending that translation.

5. **Cross-venue lead-lag (`X2`) shows an isolated 200ms-lag bump (median 0.22bps) that does not look real.** It is not confirmed at the neighboring 100ms (0.02bps) or 500ms (0.005bps) lags, and it does NOT replicate on `microstructure_reduced`'s `R6` sweep at any lag despite ~1000x more independent observations there (all `R6` effects are <0.0005bps). Read together, `X2`'s 200ms cell is the round's clearest single false-positive-by-multiple-testing example, not a real latency-diffusion window.

### microstructure_reduced (DATA_LIMITED by construction -- single day, 2026-09-01)

6. **The reduced collector looks correct.** `R1_MICROPRICE_OFFSET_L1` and `R3_BBO_IMBALANCE_L1` reproduce round-2's original L1 findings in both sign and rough order of magnitude (0.6-2.1bps gross vs round-2's 0.7-1.6bps) across all three venues and three symbols tested -- this is a useful freshness/correctness confirmation of the new collector (`futur-microstructure-reduced.service`), not a new alpha claim. `R2_OFI_TOB_L1` is the one correctness check that did NOT hold up well (weaker, sign-inconsistent vs round-2's original OFI cluster finding) and is worth re-checking once a second day of data is available, in case it's a construction gap rather than a regime difference.

7. **None of the `microstructure_reduced` numbers should be read as evidence of a stronger edge than `market_physics_v3` shows**, even though several `R1`/`R3`/`R5` gross_bps values (0.6-2.1bps) look larger than their `market_physics_v3` book-family counterparts (0.01-0.09bps). The reduced numbers come from a single calendar day and a coarser (L1-only) construction; they are directionally consistent with round-2's real-sample findings but carry none of round-2's or this round's market_physics_v3 stability evidence (no second period, no independent day). Treat them as "collector works," not "edge confirmed."

**Bottom line for the project's execution-filter priority:** this round tested the explicit hypothesis (does book state improve fill quality on real large-trade entries) directly and found nothing durable -- the best candidate (`E2`) is weak and small, the direct reframing of round-2's headline signal (`E5`) is period-unstable, and the absorption-conditioned filter (`E3`) never had enough data to test. Combined with round-2's standalone-alpha ceiling (~1.6bps gross, below cost) and this round's book-family results being another order of magnitude weaker, the microstructure family (both as standalone alpha and as an execution filter) looks close to exhausted at the L1/depth-5bps/trade-print feature set tested across rounds 1-3; a further round here would likely need either genuinely new data (order-level/L3 book, more `microstructure_reduced` history once it accumulates) or a materially different mechanism class to find anything new.
